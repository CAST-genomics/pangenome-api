Seven words for one picture

Every term below names something you can point at. The drawing is real output from the generator, not an illustration — six segments, four strands, fourteen bands.

A strand's name is three things

Every strand is identified by a triple, and the triple is the whole of its identity. A haplotype drawn as several bands is still one strand.

The words

What each term points at, and the spelling it replaces
TermWhat it is in the pictureNot
segment One of the six outlined boxes. A stretch of sequence — typically tiny, most are single-base variants. node — that word is taken
strand One coloured ribbon, end to end. One haplotype's route through the picture, identified by sample#haplotype#contig. track path walk
band One piece of one strand — a straight run or a single curve. The atomic drawable. A band count counts shapes, not genomes. ribbon — reads as the whole strand

Why the rejected words are listed

Each one is correct somewhere else, which is exactly why it causes trouble. A GFA W line calls a strand a walk; a P line and vg's JSON call it a path; tubemap.js and the attributes in the emitted document call it a track; the biology calls it a haplotype. Five spellings for one concept, all of them right inside their own file format.

The rule is that each stays correct inside the tool that uses it, and stops at this codebase's boundary. That is why the document still carries trackID and trackName — those are attribute names in a format a client already parses — while everything we write about them says strand.

The one that costs the most is node. Both granularities appear in the same URL: minigraphnode names the coarse one, and the picture it returns is made of the fine ones. That collision is the reason the distinction is written down rather than left to context.
